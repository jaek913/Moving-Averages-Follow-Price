## PASS 1 — Look-ahead

**Finding 1.1 — MINOR — attacks §5.2/B.4 drift adjustment (LB-005, LB-006).** The drift-adjusted null uses p_up = fraction of *all* daily returns that are positive, computed over the full sample. For events early in the sample, the null probability incorporates returns that occur decades later. This is look-ahead in the inferential null, not in event selection — it cannot manufacture events, but it means the drift-adjusted significance flips (2 of 3 SMA-200 repulsions "flip to random"; the 3 Hull-50 attractions survive) are conditioned on full-sample drift the trader at time t could not know. An expanding or era-local drift estimate is the causal construction. Conclusions likely survive, but LB-006's headline ("only Nikkei retains significance") rests on a non-causal null.

**Finding 1.2 — MINOR — attacks §5.6/B (IS/OOS decomposition).** The manuscript never states whether the second-half decomposition recalibrates its expanding threshold from the half boundary or inherits the full-history expanding threshold. Either is causal at the bar level, but they are different operators producing different event sets, and B does not pin which one generated LB-020. Specify it.

Everything else checked clean: the expanding percentile threshold (B.3 step 4a) is causal with min_history; era binning uses a span rule with full-history causal thresholds; the rolling windows recalibrate from scratch within-window; PE-vol's expanding rank is causal and the IS/OOS split of an already-causal series introduces no leakage (B.6 argues this correctly); the Schelling neighbor comparison applies identical causal operators to popular and neighbor windows.

## PASS 2 — Index/row alignment

Neither B.1's "futures roll handling" note nor the data-variability schema addresses non-positive closes. claims.lock asserts nan_gap_handling_explicit: true for every CL-touching claim, but the *manuscript* is silent; the replication protocol (B) is supposed to be complete, and a replicator hitting this bar has no instruction. State the handling (drop, clip, exclusion window) and confirm CL's 2020 events were inspected.

**Finding 2.2 — MINOR — attacks §4.1/§B.1 instrument identity (HSI rows).** The core table lists HSI as ticker HKEX:HSI (cash index), but the pinned file is HKEX_DLY_HSI1! — the front-month future — and SOURCES' own session schema lists "HSI front-month" under ETH futures. §4.1 classifies HSI as the cash "Hang Seng Index" under equities. Splice gaps therefore (a) can trigger qualifying events and (b) contribute pure-mechanical closure to events they fall inside, biasing futures aggregates *toward* the thesis. The pattern check the pass asks for: futures do not visibly dominate the top of the S_W range (NDX/SMA-200 is the max), so there is no smoking gun, but the dismissal as written is unjustified; a back-adjusted-series robustness check or a roll-window exclusion is the right answer.

**Finding 2.4 — MINOR — attacks §4.1 SPX early history (LB-001–004 SPX rows, LB-017, LB-019).** Pre-1926 SPX bars are monthly but treated as daily-equivalent bars: "Hull-50" there is a ~50-month filter and "H=63" is ~5 years. ## PASS 3 — Statistical inference validity

**Finding 3.1 — MINOR — attacks §5.7/B.6 (LB-009).** The 21-bar spacing makes the forward-vol windows non-overlapping, but it does not make the pairs independent. PE[t] is an expanding percentile of a 63-day rolling mean — consecutive sampled PE values share 42 of 63 underlying days and the series is near-unit-root persistent; forward vol is itself autocorrelated across adjacent quarters (vol persistence is the paper's own explanation). Spearman parametric p-values assume i.i.d. pairs, and the permutation test shuffles one persistent series against another persistent series — the textbook recipe for anti-conservative p-values under joint serial dependence. The conclusion (ρ ≈ +0.54, 11/11 positive) almost certainly survives a block permutation or effective-sample correction, but the printed p-values ("below 2×10⁻⁶", "p < 0.0002") and the "all 38 significant after Bonferroni" claim are not valid as constructed. Fix: block-permutation (block length ≥ 63 bars) or HAC-style effective-N adjustment; report corrected significance.

**Finding 3.2 — MINOR — attacks §5.5 (LB-018) and §5.2 (LB-005) multiplicity.** The Bonferroni-38 family covers PE-vol only. The direction-test battery (11 instruments × 2 specs × plain/drift-adjusted, plus 4 era cells, plus 4 quarterly-reversion horizons × 3 instruments) runs ≥60 additional tests with no family-wise accounting. The modern-era attraction (p = 0.0089) and the three Hull-50 attractions are interpreted as findings at uncorrected thresholds. Note the asymmetry: these findings cut against the thesis, so correcting them strengthens the paper's null narrative — but then the abstract's "mild attraction on three" should be stated as not surviving family-wise correction, or corrected explicitly. Either way the current accounting is incoherent: corrected where the result helps (PE-vol), uncorrected where it doesn't matter to the thesis.

**Finding 3.3 — MINOR — attacks §5.9 (LB-010).** The 137 Schelling δ's are heavily cross-dependent (same instrument across 4 popular windows and 4 timeframes; neighbor sets overlap in underlying bars), so the t-test's SE assumes far more effective observations than exist. A null with understated SE is still a null — the conclusion stands — but §5.9's "detection threshold of this 137-combination battery" language overstates the battery's power. State effective dependence or cluster by instrument.

**Finding 3.4 — MINOR — attacks §5.1 (LB-001/003).** No standard errors or CIs accompany any per-combination aggregate S_W. The paper's own synthetic battery shows path-level SD ≈ 13.9 pp on 25,000-bar series; short-history instruments (6E, 6J, FTSE: ~6,400–8,600 bars, ≲100 events) have aggregate-S_W sampling errors plausibly of 20–30 pp. "All 44 exceed 50%" is a point-estimate count presented with the rhetorical weight of a test. Given Theorem 2 the null *center* is 100%, so the count is unsurprising — but then it should be presented with uncertainty, not as 44 independent confirmations.

The paper correctly avoids t-tests on the non-normal individual-S_W distribution (reports aggregate + median; Remark 2 handles the Jensen gap honestly). The quarterly-reversion test uses non-overlapping blocks with exact t — clean.

## PASS 4 — Does the specification test the thesis?

**Finding 4.1 — LOAD-BEARING — attacks the 50% falsifier itself (Abstract; §5.1; §6.1; §7; framing of LB-001/LB-002).** The decomposition measures shares of *gap closure*, and under the paper's own algebra the falsification bar is unreachable by any economically realistic reversion process — which means clearing the bar carries almost no evidential weight against genuine attraction. Concretely: take the paper's own observed attraction, a ~53% daily toward-rate. A persistent 3 pp directional asymmetry is an enormous economic effect — roughly the edge of a top-decile systematic strategy — yet over H=63 it closes perhaps 20–40% of a typical gap through price motion, leaving aggregate S_W ≈ 70–90%. So the metric classifies an economically first-order genuine reversion force as "predominantly mechanical." Conversely, S_W < 50% requires price alone to close ≥ half the displacement within 63 trading days — stronger sustained reversion than anything in the Fama-French/Poterba-Summers literature. The thesis as operationalized ("mechanical share ≥ 50%, falsified below") is therefore approximately equivalent to "log prices are not violently mean-reverting at quarterly horizons," which was never in dispute. The decomposition is a valid and useful *accounting identity*; it is not a test that can adjudicate between "no attraction" and "modest-but-economically-important attraction," and the paper's interpretive superstructure (§6.1: the decomposition "can help determine whether the correlation reflects genuine price dynamics"; §7: "largely a visual artifact") treats it as one. The direction test — not S_W — is the instrument that bears on genuine attraction, and it found attraction (3 instruments; modern era). The paper must either (a) reframe the contribution as a decomposition/attribution identity plus a calibration of how large attraction must be to move S_W, with an explicit power statement (what toward-rate would push aggregate S_W below 50%?), or (b) defend the 50% bar as economically meaningful, which I do not believe it can.

**Finding 4.2 — MINOR — attacks Theorem 2's coverage of the 44-cell headline.** Theorem 2 requires H ≥ N (window disjointness drives the Markov step). SMA-200 at H=63 — 11 of 44 cells, including the global maximum (NDX 166.8%) — violates it; the two SMA windows share 137 bars and the conditional-moment derivation fails. Corollary 1 discloses this and defers to synthetic controls — but §5.3 reports synthetic aggregates as single numbers without stating which MA spec(s) the synthetic battery ran. If the 100.57%/99.1% null calibration is Hull-50-only (or pooled), the SMA-200@63 cells have neither a proved nor a spec-matched empirical null, and "exceeds 100%" for those cells is uncalibrated. State the per-spec synthetic nulls.

**Finding 4.3 — MINOR.** S_W > 100% (33/44) is evidence of *continuation/momentum* within the window, not additional evidence for mechanical adaptation; the abstract's parenthetical explains this correctly but §5.1's framing ("consistent with the mathematical prediction") blurs that >100% is a drift/momentum signature, not a filter-mechanics one — under the zero-drift null the center is 100%, and systematically exceeding it is a statement about trends.

## PASS 5 — Interpretation overreach

**Finding 5.1 — MINOR — Abstract & §7: "The one finding with forward-looking predictive content is the PE-volatility correlation."** False by the paper's own §5.8: the SPX quarterly reversion (r = −0.278 at 126 days, non-overlapping blocks) is a forward *return* predictor — forward-looking predictive content by any definition. Rewrite ("the one finding with predictive content at the horizons where adaptation dominates," or similar).

**Finding 5.2 — MINOR — Abstract omissions.** The abstract reports "all 44 exceed 50%" and full-sample direction nulls but omits (i) the five sub-50% rolling windows including a negative one (1991–2001, −8.0%) spanning much of 1986–2016, and (ii) the modern-era significant attraction (53.1%, p = 0.0089). Both are material qualifications to "predominantly," both are disclosed honestly in §5.5–5.6, and both belong in the abstract's qualification sentence alongside the quarterly-reversion caveat.

**Finding 5.3 — MINOR — §7 "largely a visual artifact" and §2.1 "leaving little residual for any genuine price attraction to explain."** Both exceed what S_W supports given Finding 4.1: a 64–90% mechanical share leaves a 10–36% residual that, expressed as a daily directional edge, is economically large, and the paper's own modern-era and 3-instrument attraction findings live in that residual. "Visual artifact" is hedged with "may be," but the artifact is in the *interpretation* of the chart, not the chart — price genuinely reaches the MA. Soften both, or anchor them explicitly to the direction-test nulls rather than to S_W.

**Finding 5.4 — NO ISSUE on PE-vol causal language.** §5.7/§6.3 are careful: predictive framing, explicit attribution to known vol clustering, "novel metric for a known mechanism." The only PE-vol overreach is inferential (Finding 3.1), not causal.

## PASS 6 — Is the gap claim real?

**NO LOAD-BEARING FINDING, with two citation obligations.** From my knowledge plus targeted search: I cannot identify prior work that formally decomposes price–trailing-MA gap closure into price and filter shares with a falsifiable share threshold. The adjacent territory is occupied but not this cell: Shintani-Yabu-Nagakura (2012) is spurious signal significance, not closure attribution; Zakamulin-Giner (2020) is rule correlation; Franses (1991) is autocorrelation inflation — all cited. Two relevant uncited traditions: (a) Working (1960, Econometrica) — time-averaging of a random chain mechanically induces spurious autocorrelation in averaged series; this is the one-sided-filter artifact result in economics predating the paper's claimed extension of the two-sided critique, and it weakens the framing that the causal-filter side "has received less attention" without acknowledgment. (b) Miller, Muthuswamy & Whaley (1994, JF) — "Mean Reversion of S&P 500 Index Basis Changes: Arbitrage-Induced or Statistical Illusion?" — a direct precedent for the paper's genre (apparent financial mean reversion shown to be a statistical/mechanical construction artifact), as is the Kim-Nelson-Startz (1991, REStud) reappraisal line. Citing these strengthens rather than threatens the gap claim, but their absence overstates the novelty of the critique tradition (the novelty of the decomposition survives). Both findings MINOR.

## Verdict

**Certify after fixes.** The mechanical core is sound and unusually well-disclosed: the algebra is correct, the decomposition is a valid identity, the synthetic calibration is the right control, and the rebuild-reconciliation candor (B.14) is exemplary. But one finding is LOAD-BEARING: the 50% falsifier cannot discriminate the economically interesting alternative — the paper's own observed 53% toward-rates would leave S_W comfortably above 50% — so the interpretive claims built on clearing that bar (§6.1's adjudication claim, §7's "visual artifact", §2.1's "little residual") are not supported by the metric and must be reframed around what the decomposition actually establishes (an attribution identity plus the direction-test nulls), ideally with an explicit power calibration: the toward-rate required to drive aggregate S_W below 50% at H=63. The MINOR findings requiring action before certification: CL negative-close handling disclosed (2.1), HSI identity corrected (2.2), roll-splice robustness substantiated (2.3), the ex-1926 robustness claim registered or printed (2.4), PE-vol inference corrected for serial dependence (3.1), multiplicity accounting made coherent (3.2), per-spec synthetic nulls stated (4.2), and the abstract/§7 sentences in 5.1–5.3 revised. Because Finding 4.1 is LOAD-BEARING, a second review round is triggered upon the author's response.

## Finding 4.1 (LOAD-BEARING) — FIX+REBUT

**Rebuttal of the claim as stated.** The finding asserts the 50% bar is "unreachable by any economically realistic reversion process" and is "approximately equivalent to 'log prices are not violently mean-reverting.'" That is checkable, and it is false. A committed calibration (`analysis/falsifier_calibration.py`, output `analysis/outputs/falsifier_calibration.json`, MD5 `b34cfe1cc421f8cd9269352467b3a880`, deterministic on both machines) drives the paper's own unmodified `decompose()` operator over synthetic paths with an explicit, tunable daily attraction toward a trailing mean, and measures the resulting aggregate S_W at H=63 (Hull-50, 75th-pct expanding threshold). Result: aggregate S_W is **monotone decreasing in genuine attraction** and **crosses 50% at attraction coefficient β ≈ 0.087** (S_W: β=0 → ~92–100%; β=0.03 → 58%; β=0.08 → 52%; β=0.12 → 42%; β=0.20 → 33%). The falsifier is therefore reachable by a finite, identifiable amount of genuine reversion; the metric is responsive, not slack. The reviewer's specific "53% toward-rate → S_W 70–90%" example actually cuts the other way once computed: in the calibration the realized toward-rate is **pinned at 64–71% across the whole β range** by the threshold/overlap measurement structure — i.e. the toward-rate barely moves while S_W sweeps from 100% to 33%, which is exactly why the paper does not rest the headline on the toward-rate.

**Concession of the actionable half.** The reviewer is nonetheless right that (a) the toward-rate is a weak discriminator and should not be leaned on, and (b) three interpretive sentences claim more than the S_W identity alone licenses. These are FIXED in the manuscript: §7's "largely a visual artifact" and §2.1's "leaving little residual" are reanchored to the direction-test nulls rather than to S_W, and a new paragraph in §5.4 reports the falsifier calibration explicitly (the β≈0.087 crossing and the toward-rate insensitivity), so the paper now states what the bar can and cannot discriminate. The contribution is reframed in §6.1 from "determines whether the correlation reflects genuine price dynamics" to "is an attribution identity that bounds the mechanical share; the direction test, not S_W, adjudicates the existence of attraction." Net effect: the thesis (mechanical share > 50% in aggregate) stands and is now shown to be a genuine, passable-or-failable test; the over-claim around it is removed.
